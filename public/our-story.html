Two families, one crepe counter

Cafeto 21 is the Tacoma chapter of a family recipe that starts with a coffee shop in Mexico and a crepe cart on the street.

The counter behind the counter

Owner Esteban Regla and his cousin Juan Pablo decided to go into business together after moving to Washington five years ago. Juan Pablo had owned a coffee shop in Mexico and sold crepes from a street cart — the folding hand that built the menu you see today.

Their first shop was Redondo Coffee & Crepes in Renton — same coffee bar, same crepe press, same make-your-own line. When the pair went looking for a second home, downtown Tacoma fit. They moved into the old Eather Tea space on Broadway — the tea-and-dessert shop next to the theater had closed at the end of 2025 — and opened Cafeto 21 in June 2026, with the crepe emphasis front and center.

Why the crepes lead

The name reads like a coffee bar, but the menu says otherwise: the sweet-and-savory crepe counter is the reason people cross town, and the espresso bar attached to it is the reason they come back at 8 a.m. on a Tuesday. The shop holds five stars on the review feeds so far. The matcha and the Horchata Latte do their own recruiting.

The name, said right

“Caf-ay-to,” the way Esteban says it — Spanish name, French crepes, Tacoma address.

Questions people ask

When did you open?
In June 2026. The family behind Redondo Coffee & Crepes in Renton moved into the old Eather Tea space on Broadway and opened Cafeto 21 — the menus mirror each other, with the crepe emphasis.
What are the hours?
8 a.m. to 4 p.m., every day of the week — seven-day hours on Broadway. The details are on the visit page.
Where exactly are you?
1127 Broadway, Suite 101 — between S 11th and S 13th, next to Tacoma Dance Studios and the Tacoma School of the Arts theater.
Can I work here?
That’s what a lot of people do — free Wi-Fi, a spacious, bright interior, and the shop sits right beside the School of the Arts.
Can I get it delivered?
Yes — the full menu is on DoorDash for delivery and pickup in Tacoma.
Is it caf-AY-to or caf-EE-to?
“Caf-ay-to,” the way owner Esteban Regla says it. The name is Spanish; the crepes are French; the home is Tacoma.
